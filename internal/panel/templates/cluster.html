How to restore {{if ne $d.Tenant "TENANT"}}{{$d.Tenant}}{{else}}a tenant{{end}} from a backup

A restore puts a backup in place of a tenant's data, on the node that runs it{{with $d.RestoreNode}} ({{.}}){{end}}. The panel does not run it: it needs the backups' private key, which stays off the cluster.

{{if not $d.Newest.IsZero}}Its newest backup is from {{utc $d.Newest}}{{if not $d.Chosen}}, used below{{end}}. Step 1 lists the others.{{end}}
{{with $d.AtProblem}}
{{.}}
{{end}}
  1. On {{if $d.RestoreNode}}{{$d.RestoreNode}}{{else}}the node that runs it{{end}}, list its backups, to check there is one at or before that time: 
    sudo murder backups list {{$d.Tenant}}
  2. On {{if $d.RestoreNode}}{{$d.RestoreNode}}{{else}}that node{{end}}, archive it, so nothing writes to its data meanwhile; it stops serving: 
    sudo murder tenant archive {{$d.Tenant}}
  3. Bring the backups' private key (key.txt) to {{if $d.RestoreNode}}{{$d.RestoreNode}}{{else}}that node{{end}} for this step only, and restore its data from the newest backup at or before {{if $d.RestoreAt.IsZero}}that time{{else}}{{utc $d.RestoreAt}}{{end}}. That backup may be older than the time chosen (step 1 shows which it is): everything written since that backup is replaced. 
    sudo murder backups restore {{$d.Tenant}} -at {{if $d.RestoreAt.IsZero}}TIME{{else}}{{iso $d.RestoreAt}}{{end}} -identity key.txt
     Then remove the key from the node.
  4. Run it again: 
    sudo murder tenant restore {{$d.Tenant}}
  5. Check it here: its state and logs. What it had stays beside it, unmounted (murder-replaced-…), until you remove it.

For another tenant, open it from its page's "Restore".
